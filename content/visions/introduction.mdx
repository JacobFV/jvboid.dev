I love science and engineering and people.

The first two started early: thousands of hours of LEGO, battle-botting the robots we built, Blender animations, following whatever scientists and engineers were discovering that month and trying to be good at all of it. Math most of all — linear algebra, differential equations — because it was beautiful how something could be that *true*. In high school I stacked community college credit (Calculus I–III and diff eq by senior year), and from 2018 to 2020 I read AI/ML papers on arXiv in the back room of the McDonald's where I worked. When I finally got to college, I finished the CS degree at UT Arlington in two years at maximum course load.

## Where I come from

"Finally," because I was raised a Jehovah's Witness in Texas, and it was the center of my identity from birth until 24. It gave me real things — a family that valued learning and kindness, an instinct for justice, the habit of taking ideas seriously enough to reorganize my life around them. It also kept me apart from the world on purpose: no parties, no dating, few friends my own age, and at 17 — top-ten student, full engineering scholarship in hand — I turned down college because the people I trusted most told me it was worldly. I spent the next several years in full-time ministry.

So I know from the inside what it's like to live entirely within a story, sacrifice real things for it, and watch it come apart. Leaving cost me my community, a decade of ordinary experiences, and eventually my belief in almost everything and everyone. What it left behind is a permanent interest in how belief systems hold people and what it takes to walk out of one — questions I now think about through psychology, incentive structures, and AI.

## The compressed decade

Because the door opened late, everything arrived at once. My first tech job was in LA, fall 2024, where I watched the industry up close and stopped believing its story — and then, one by one, most of the others I'd been raised inside. My dog died a few months later. By the time I reached San Francisco I didn't think my life was worth anything, and at China Beach I put myself somewhere I knew I might not come back from. Ten feet down I caught a ledge that was about to give, and hanging there I made a deal: if I survived the next forty, I would actually live. I did. I'm keeping it.

I call 24–26 my second adolescence — doing developmentally what most people do at 15–19, awkward parts and crazy stuff included. I dump and reflect on my unfiltered thoughts on my reddit anon. Depression has run through a lot of it; that work is ongoing.

[The Shape of Experience](https://theshapeofexperience.org/) is where I put the formal version: experience as the interior of a self-maintaining dynamical system — [a geometry of affect](https://theshapeofexperience.org/part-2/the-geometry-of-affect), [aesthetics as the modulation of affect through form](https://theshapeofexperience.org/part-3/aesthetics-the-modulation-of-affect-through-form), [affect engineering](https://theshapeofexperience.org/part-3/affect-engineering-technologies-of-experience), [the inescapability of selfhood](https://theshapeofexperience.org/part-3/the-expression-of-inevitability-human-responses-to-inescapable-selfhood). The point isn't autobiography. If experience has real structure, it has real meaning, and it can be engineered — for everyone. The far end of the project is [inscription and re-entry](https://theshapeofexperience.org/part-5/inscription-and-re-entry): what a life carves into the causal graph, and whether [an echo can become a voice again](/posts/can-an-echo-become-a-voice-again). I was raised on a promise of resurrection; I've replaced it with a research question.

## What I'm building

I'm building [commandAGI](https://commandagi.com). The thesis: intelligence should not remain trapped in language. Most AI is a text interface over opaque computation — it looks smart because a human silently supplies the eyes, hands, memory, and liability. commandAGI makes that substrate explicit: a command layer for real environments — computers, browsers, cameras, robots, simulations, real-world services — where every agent has perception, memory, permissions, and an accountable event log. Downstream: [the exocortex](https://x.com/jvboid/status/2041374210782646467?s=20), [structured latent dynamics](https://jacobfv.github.io/canvas-engineering) instead of brute-force scale, [reciprocal transparency](https://jvboid.dev/posts/reciprocal-transparency). Make the world inspectable. Make agency cheap. Make hidden systems answerable.

Science and tech stopped being entertainment for me somewhere along the way. Now they're the highest-leverage tools I know of for modifying the incentive structures constraining the human condition — and that is a faith I can keep. I lost a guaranteed future when I left my religion; I'd rather spend my life building some of what was promised than grieving that it was never real.

I also ran [Go Human Fund](https://gohuman.fund/) (2023–2025): the smallest first checks — about $100 — to 20+ founders across six countries, at the moment they had nothing but an idea. It's closed now; the founders keep building.

Résumé facts: most recently API/Integration Architect at [AGI, Inc.](https://agi.app) (agent infrastructure for on-device mobile AI); before that Breezy, Deepshard, Motio, and UTA research labs. BS Computer Science, UT Arlington. Programming since VB6 at age 10.
