## Real iOS. Not a lookalike.

An emulated iPhone 11 boots Apple's unmodified iOS 14 on an ARM Linux server. The lock screen, SpringBoard, Setup Assistant, Clock, Settings, Safari: the operating system itself, with nothing re-implemented. A rack of devices can't be snapshotted or scaled. Apple's Simulator runs only on a Mac. This runs where agents are trained.

## Your apps. Installed for real.

Apps are compiled on Linux against UIKit, packaged as IPAs, signed and staged into the device image, then launched by iOS like anything from the App Store. A toolchain built for this compiles storyboards, nibs and asset catalogs too, so open-source apps from GitHub come across with no Mac involved.

## Driven by an agent. Checked like an experiment.

Taps and swipes go in through a virtual touchscreen. Every action is captured before and after, reviewed, and recorded with hashes and transcripts. A run that cannot be checked does not count.

<Topology3D id="iphone-on-linux" caption="Firmware and apps in. An emulated iPhone running real iOS. An agent's taps through a control channel, frames back, and a record of all of it." />

## Online.

The phone reaches the internet over a USB-Ethernet link into a small Linux bridge that routes its frames and answers its DNS. Safari loads Wikipedia and Google over HTTPS. Weather shows live data.

## Two at once.

Two emulated iPhones completed the same strict journey together on one machine, then idled using **64.6% of one CPU core** and **10.76 GiB of memory** between them. A measured pair, not a limit.

## Built in seven days. After a week spent the wrong way.

The first attempt re-implemented iOS system libraries on a CPU emulator and reached zero percent parity with a real device. The lesson was to stop imitating the operating system and run the real one. What this project adds, on top of ChefKiss's [Inferno](https://github.com/ChefKissInc/Inferno) fork of QEMU, is everything an iPhone needs to be useful on a server: firmware prepared into bootable images, apps built and imported, a headless control layer, and evidence for every action. It exists as ground truth for CommandAGI's verification infrastructure and for [Computerworld](/projects/computerworld), a simulated computer world that includes an iOS shell.

## Tech specs.

| | |
|---|---|
| Host | aarch64 Linux, CPU emulation only. Two virtual CPUs and 4 GB of memory per guest. |
| Firmware | Apple's iOS 14.0 beta 5 restore image, supplied by the user. The tools stage it and never download, decrypt or redistribute Apple's code. |
| Apps | Objective-C built with clang against hand-written UIKit headers; Swift and SwiftUI built on Linux or by Xcode. Ad-hoc signed IPAs. App Store binaries are rejected by design. |
| Control | A QMP channel into the emulator, a virtual multitouch device, and a headless display read back as frames. |
| Evidence | Commands, control and display transcripts, timings, before and after screenshots with hashes, and a review of whether the screen changed the way the action claimed. |
| Known limits | The display intermittently freezes (about 45% of app sessions on 30 October; each costs a fresh boot). Setup takes one to three minutes per screen. Maps and Stocks quit at launch. General App Store compatibility is not claimed. |
