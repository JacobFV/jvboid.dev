- **The idea:** free, local-first tools for designing circuit boards, and engineers for hire when a team needs the board actually done. Software as the funnel, a service as the business
- **What existed:** a browser workbench that really edits schematics and boards, with reviewed, undoable changes; a marketing site, pricing, sales PDFs and a launch film
- **What happened:** no clients. Looking back at the repository, it is easy to see why: nobody could have become one

## Problem

Small hardware teams (embedded, robotics, IoT) lose weeks to board design that isn't their product. The tools lock a design into one vendor's file format, the reasoning behind each change lives in someone's head, and "the design-rule check is green" gets mistaken for "this board is ready to fabricate." In the summer of 2026 I wanted to sell the fix as a service: scope a board, engineer it, hand back an open project file with the evidence for every decision.

## Solution

GenieEDA was three tiers. **genieeda**, a free workspace that runs in the browser with no account, keeps one open project file, and turns every edit into a reviewed, journaled diff. **genie engineering**, paid board design: a pilot board for $5k–15k, team capacity at $25k–50k a year, every number marked as an initial hypothesis. And **genie lab**, an office fab-lab kit with a robot, which was an idea and a page.

## How

Almost all of it was built in about fifteen hours on 27–28 September, and mostly not by me typing. I wrote a 77-task backlog and a master prompt, and Codex and Claude subagents worked through it in parallel, about seventy-five git worktrees, 356 commits. I reviewed, merged and steered.

- **Code:** a TypeScript monorepo of about 117,000 lines in 25 packages, plus a Rust/WASM polygon kernel for board geometry
- **Apps:** the website, the browser workbench (IndexedDB storage, a service worker, works offline), an unsigned Electron shell, and a pipeline that renders the launch film from scripted, real-time captures of the workbench
- **Hosting:** one Cloudflare Worker serving static assets, with 17 D1 migrations for identity, sync, jobs, reviews and an engineering portal, almost all of it behind a "planned release" flag
- **AI:** six typed tools an agent could use to propose edits, each applied only after a person approves the diff. They were tested against a scripted fake model, never a real one

## Results

Zero clients, and the repository explains it better than I can. The intake form was switched off (`INTAKE_ENABLED=0`). `robots.txt` disallowed every page and the footer said "not indexed, no analytics", so nobody could find it. All three outbound email sequences sat at `approved: false`. The disclosures page said, accurately, that no engagement had been delivered and no legal entity or contact address had been published. No board was ever fabricated.

## Lessons

I built the product and the brochure and skipped the only part a business can't skip: asking someone to pay. Agents made it cheap to produce a convincing-looking company in a night, which made it easy to keep polishing the company instead of talking to a single hardware team. The workbench is a decent narrow tool. The next time I try to sell something, the first artifact will be a conversation, not a pricing page.
