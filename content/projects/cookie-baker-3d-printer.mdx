- A programmable 12"×12" XY gantry, built at 13 as an attempted 3D printer
- The motion platform worked; the extruder was never added, so it never printed anything
- Re-attempted in October 2014 with design modifications
- Its name was later handed down to the [Cookie Cutter CNC](/projects/cookie-cutter-cnc), the 36"×36" sheet-metal machine I built in its honor

## Problem

I wanted a 3D printer and could not buy one. In 2013 a hobby printer
still cost more than everything I owned, so the obvious move was to
build one — starting, sensibly enough, with the part that moves.

## Solution

A programmable 12"×12" two-axis gantry: a threadscrew XY table driven by
stepper motors under Arduino control, sized so a print bed and an
extruder could sit on top of it later. It reached the point where it
would trace a commanded path across its working area.

![The Cookie Baker 3D printer build](/img/migrated/cookie_baker.jpg)

## Results

The gantry moved. The extruder never got built, so the Cookie Baker
never baked anything — it ended its life as a 2D plotter that had been
designed for a third axis it never grew. I revisited the design in
October 2014 with modifications, and made a
[3D animation of it rotating](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MvlUK8JUYY)
to show the mechanism.

## Lessons

This was where I learned that the interesting part of a machine is
rarely the part you start with. Motion platforms are tractable —
steppers, leadscrews, a controller loop. The extruder, with its
thermal control and filament feed, was the actual project, and I had
budgeted my attention as though it were an accessory.

Two years later I built the [Cookie Cutter CNC](/projects/cookie-cutter-cnc)
— a 36"×36" sheet-metal CNC with a full C# toolpath stack — and named it
in honor of this one. That machine is where the software, electronics,
and the electro-discharge cutting head story live; the Cookie Baker is
just the gantry that came first.
