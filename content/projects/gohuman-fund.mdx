## Problem

The earliest stage of building is the stage nobody funds. Before a demo,
before a team, before a deck, a founder has an idea and their own
determination — and the entire capital stack, down to pre-seed, is
structured to wait until someone else has already taken the risk. The
scarce thing at that point isn't money. It's the first person willing to
say the idea is worth acting on.

## Solution

Go Human Fund (formerly ComputaCombinator LLC, Delaware) was a micro-seed
fund that ran from 2023 to 2025 and wrote the smallest first checks I
could actually write: about $100, to founders at the very beginning. Not
enough to build anything with. Enough to be the first outside party on
record backing them, which is what the check was really for.

## How

- **Check size:** ~$100 initial investment per founder.
- **Terms:** negotiated per project rather than templated — some equity,
  some not, depending on what fit the founder's situation.
- **Capital:** personally financed. No LPs, no fundraise, no committee.
- **Reach:** 20+ founders across 6 countries.
- **Portfolio:** Sodium BMS (sodium battery science for cost-effective EV
  BMS networks, Vibhuti Bafna), Leapflow (AI agents for workflow
  automation, Suhas Sumukh), Zip Notary (remote online notarization SaaS,
  Ray McDonald), among others.

## Results

The founders outran the fund, which was the point:

- **Advik Kooper** raised from Peter Thiel's 1517 Fund for Exerton, a
  Solana platform for young founders.
- **Sur Naik** received a $20,000 grant from Emergent Ventures for his
  electronics prototyping system.
- **Emma Obadoni** built Tech Family, a 50+ member community teaching
  technology across Africa.
- **Pablo Perez** and **Khadija Challoey** went on to machine learning and
  physics research respectively.

The fund closed in April 2025 after completing its initial mission. The
site now points founders who are still looking toward the funds that back
people at that stage — 1517, Emergent Ventures, YC, Village Global,
Pioneer, Founders Fund.

## Lessons

A personally financed fund has exactly one hard ceiling, and it is the
person financing it. Closing was the honest move: the alternative was
raising from LPs, which would have replaced the thing that made the fund
work — no committee, no thesis, no minimum viable traction — with the
thing every other fund already is. Some of what I learned running it was
harder than that; watching what happened to a few of the people I was
supporting is part of why I stopped believing several stories I'd been
told about how this industry works. The conviction underneath it hasn't
changed. Being the first to say "I believe in you" costs almost nothing
and is worth far more than $100.
