SC-WBD is a working model of **one person's brain** — 414 regions wired the way theirs are, carrying what is known about each one's chemistry, myelination and timescale. Electrical activity every millisecond, blood flow every two seconds, a magnetic pulse pushing on the tissue, and what the person actually does: each reads the **same shared state**, through its own physics, on its own clock.

## The World's First Whole Brain Dynamics (WBD) Model

<Video
  url="https://pub-045364f0fab44827afa95b1e44b6e18d.r2.dev/scwbd-overview.mp4"
  title="What SC-WBD is, and what it is for, 52 seconds"
/>

Every way of looking at a brain sees something different. Electrodes read it a thousand times a second but can barely say where the signal came from. An MRI scanner sees the whole brain but reads it once every two seconds, and reads blood flow rather than activity. A magnetic pulse or a beam of focused ultrasound goes the other way, pushing on the tissue instead of watching it — ultrasound reaching structures deep enough that no non-invasive instrument can otherwise touch them. Additionally, what a person looks at, or types, or says is not a brain measurement at all, and yet even that information can inform our understanding of a given brain.

Most people are only ever measured one way, so results from each instrument never quite add up, and almost none of them are about any particular brain. SC-WBD keeps one shared model and lets each kind of signal constrain the part it can actually speak to.

![Four kinds of signal around one brain: going in, reading the brain, coming out, and the background conditions enclosing all of it](/assets/img/projects/sc-wbd-signals.svg)

Each way of reading the brain gets its own forward model, because each one measures a different physical quantity. Electrical recording is a **lead field** computed from a boundary-element head model, which carries regional current to each scalp electrode. fMRI is the **Balloon–Windkessel** haemodynamic differential equation, integrated forward so blood-flow signal is *predicted* from neural state rather than assumed proportional to it. fNIRS is a photon-path model through scalp, skull and cortex. What the person does is read at the boundary of the body and needs no head model at all.[^1]

They also run on **different clocks, and are not resampled onto a common one**. The electrical head reads the latent trajectory every millisecond; the haemodynamic head reads the same trajectory once a second or two, through an equation whose own time constants are seconds long. Nothing is interpolated into fictitious equivalence between them — the shared state is what they have in common, and the operators are where they differ. That is what the word *integrated* is doing in this project's name, and it is also where run 4's fMRI result went wrong: the equation integrates now, and the likelihood it produces still degrades over training. ([scwbd/observe/\_\_init\_\_.py](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/scwbd/observe/__init__.py))

None of that starts from nothing. Before any signal arrives the model is conditioned on a measured prior over the same 414 regions: **12,274 tracts** between them — 1,618 taken as hard structure, 7,386 soft, 3,270 proposed — at a mean length of 38.8 mm, and **33 maps per region**, among them a 19-tracer PET panel of receptor and transporter densities, T1w/T2w myelin, cortical thickness, MEG intrinsic timescale, the principal functional gradients, the sensorimotor–association axis, and cerebral blood flow and glucose metabolism. Every region the model has an opinion about therefore arrives already carrying what is known about its chemistry, its myelination, its timescale and its wiring, and the posterior is conditioned on all of it rather than on the recordings alone. ([reports/anatomy\_prior.md](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/reports/anatomy_prior.md)) The prior is also published on its own, and each map carries its own licence and citation.

Sorting signals this way is what makes partial data usable.[^2] Someone's gaze is real evidence, but it is not a reading of their cortex, and a model that confuses the two learns the wrong thing.

## New: bWIN EEG 001 and a matched comparison

A task-specific WIN model forecasts 64 future EEG samples from 24 observed samples. On 25 held-out people it improves over zero, while fitted linear and community neural alternatives have lower average error. Both SC-WBD-003 and SC-WBD-004 perform better on the five people held out by both checkpoints. This is a measured comparison of separate research artifacts, not a claim that the new model supersedes the integrated predecessors.

[Read the results, actual-data figure, and comparisons with released models](https://sc-wbd.pages.dev/releases/bwin-eeg-001.html) · [Checkpoint and source](https://huggingface.co/brandonin/bwin-eeg-001)

## Modelling brain activity

The first thing the model does is the ordinary thing: given what a brain has been doing, say what it does next. Each of the 414 regions carries its own state and its own uncertainty, and they are wired to each other the way the real ones are, so activity in one region moves the regions it is actually connected to.

![Show someone a film and you know exactly what they saw, where they looked, and what they said about it — alongside what their brain did. Four different kinds of evidence about one response, all landing on one model.](/assets/img/projects/sc-wbd-connectome.png)

What each region carries counts for as much as how many regions there are. Summarising one of the model's 400 cortical parcels by a single number retains **32.1%** of the whitened EEG lead field; carrying its net dipole moment — three numbers instead of one — retains **83.4%**. Splitting the same parcels into eight times as many scalar pieces reaches 70.8%, and costs 3,154 numbers to the moment's 1,200.[^3] So orientation is the largest single win available and the cheapest one, and the state is built to carry it.

The prediction comes back with a confidence attached rather than as a bare number, and a value the model could not actually resolve comes back marked unresolved instead of as a plausible guess.

## Simulating intervention responses

Once a model can say what a brain does next, you can ask what it would do if you changed something. Magnetic stimulation is already used to treat depression, and where you aim it matters a great deal — but nobody can currently say what a given pulse did to the network, so targeting is done by scalp landmark and the answer arrives weeks later as a symptom score.

For example, sleep: the depth and timing of slow-wave activity is one of the better-characterised things stimulation can push on, and it is measurable the same night rather than in six weeks. A model that predicts what a pulse at a given site and phase does to that person's own network turns sleep optimisation from a protocol applied to everyone into a target chosen for one person and checked against their own recording.

> A magnetic pulse goes in; the electrical response, the blood-flow response and the muscle twitch all come back out. The field is computed through that person's own head, not an average one.

Two coil positions produce measurably different predicted EEG here, and the difference is carried by the coil's *orientation* rather than by how strong the field is.[^4] The field calculations are validated.

SC-WBD-003 trains on measured stimulation: single-pulse TMS delivered over one primary motor cortex with 64-channel EEG recording, 704 epochs from two participants. The evoked response is there in the data before any model touches it — trial-averaged global field power reaches 5.4× baseline for left-M1 pulses and 4.6× for right-M1, peaking around 160–170 ms.[^5]

The step from a computed field to a neural response remains unvalidated, and the reason is now a property of the recording rather than of the model. Turning a coil into a drive requires the coil's position and orientation; that release was MRI-navigated but ships no per-pulse pose, so there is no field to validate the map from. The drive 003 learns is anchored only by which hemisphere the muscle response identifies and by that hemisphere's motor parcels. One target site, one intensity, two participants — a pulse somewhere the model has not seen is still untested.

## Fine-tuneable for personalized neurotechnology

The last step is the one the whole programme is for. A model of the average brain is a statistic, and no device is worn by a statistic. Because every signal is attached to the part of the model it can speak to, the shared model can be fitted to one person from whatever recordings that person actually has — and the rest is left marginalised rather than filled in with someone else's anatomy.

> Reading intent from the scalp to move a cursor. What makes this hard is not decoding once; it is decoding the same way tomorrow, which is a problem about modelling a person rather than a population.

There is also a tool for asking, before anyone is scanned, what a proposed experiment could actually resolve — so a session that cannot separate two hypotheses is visible in advance rather than after. [Possibilities](https://sc-wbd.pages.dev/possibilities/index.html) sets out what this makes reachable, in the order it becomes reachable.

These scores are **not a shared-test leaderboard**: splits and preprocessing differ. bWIN's value is its 25-person primary result; its matched comparison with 003 and 004 uses five common held-out people. WBD-001 and 002 also used different target units from their baselines, making their reported scores roughly 0.6 nats too low; correcting that increases their gap. ([reports/training/eval\_metric\_incomparability.md](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/reports/training/eval_metric_incomparability.md))

**Scale moved by a factor of ten between 002 and 003, and the score moved with it.** 003 is the first model here to beat every baseline it was measured against, by ~0.04 nats. 004 is the same size, adds a stage, and **does not reproduce that separation** — on a holdout built under a different split policy, so it is a failure to reproduce on other data rather than a regression on the same data, which is the weakest of the available defences.[^6] Parameter count is the axis that grew monotonically here; the forecast score is not, and no arrangement of these four numbers makes it so.

**What did grow every run is how much of the model can be measured at all.** 002 could be scored on one thing. 004 is scored on five, and it is the first run able to say anything about fMRI, about whether its own posterior is calibrated, or about whether fitting to an individual helps — because each of those needed an instrument built before it could return an answer. Three of the five came back negative, and that is the trade this programme keeps making: a measurement that can fail is worth more than a capability nobody checked.

### Where this is heading

005 is designed from 004's evidence rather than from its disappointments, and the design is written down before the run. ([reports/RUN5\_DESIGN.md](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/reports/RUN5_DESIGN.md)) Three things are already decided:

- **The fMRI head gets fitted to a settled state instead of fighting for one.** 004's haemodynamic likelihood degrades through training because the only haemodynamic corpus is 5.39% of the gradient and is outvoted 17.6 : 1. Four diagnostic arms located the cause: freezing the five Balloon–Windkessel constants changes nothing, and freezing the shared trunk sends the likelihood **1.99 → 1.86 and still falling**. The head models this data perfectly well; it is the state underneath it that will not hold still.[^7]
- **The posterior is not re-tuned until one cheap experiment has run.** 004's learning-rate repair worked and overshot. The sweep that chose it predicted a coupling-gain R² of 0.674–0.766 and the full curriculum returned 0.284, and why is unmeasured. Scoring an intermediate checkpoint that is already on disk separates a schedule problem from a sweep that does not transfer, and it costs one evaluation.
- **Individualisation starts from the 30.** Of 75 participants scored on a second night, 30 carry a person effect of exactly zero. Whether those are the participants the stage sampled least is nearly free to check and rules out two of the three candidate causes.

The distance to a validated claim about brains is known and most of it is not compute. Five claim gates need **nine baseline arms** that were never trained — about **380 hours** of wall clock, one job at a time, to train them to a state a claim could rest on.[^8] One gate is not reachable at any budget: it requires recovery of direction, delay, gain, dose and state-dependence from a *prospective* perturbation study, and three of those five are unobtainable in principle from the retrospective stimulation data that exists. ([reports/PROGRAMME\_ASSESSMENT.md](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/reports/PROGRAMME_ASSESSMENT.md)) The new [bWIN release](https://sc-wbd.pages.dev/releases/bwin-eeg-001.html) now supplies a fresh five-person common-holdout comparison of 003 and 004. A common-split retraining remains a different, larger experiment.

Stated at the granularity someone could check, with the public artifacts in the same table as the parts they are made of. Every row is a thing you could run or download.

The validated results above are **computations**: four field solvers checked against closed-form solutions, a compiler checked for internal consistency, and an audit confirming every guard has an input under which it reads differently — so a green reading is evidence rather than decoration. What is *not* validated is any claim about brains. **All five claim gates report <span className="wbd-chip unknown">could_not_run</span>**, because the baselines each one names — naive resampling, single-modality, population, anatomy-only, session-adapted — were never trained, and one gate needs perturbation data nobody holds. A gate that could not run is not a gate that passed, and nothing on this page rests on one. ([reports/gates/SUMMARY.md](https://github.com/JacobFV/sc-wbd/blob/master/reports/gates/SUMMARY.md))
