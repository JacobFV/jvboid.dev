A hidden obstacle is introduced while the forward range sensor begins reporting that space is more open than it really is. The corruption is correlated and superficially plausible; merely smoothing the readings does not identify it.

## Signature of self-awareness

SA predicts a drop in the reliability of its own perceptual update and selects a **reference sweep**. The head motion is not the evidence by itself. The important causal sequence is

$$
\text{predicted sensor failure}
\rightarrow \text{diagnostic head sweep}
\rightarrow \text{reference observation}
\rightarrow \text{revised map and policy}.
$$

Across 20 paired evaluations, SA improves utility by $+0.266$, with 95% interval $[+0.130,+0.409]$. When the same trained SA policy has its self-model causally disabled, it fails the representative run and repeatedly collides with the hidden crate.

## Why this is the cleanest positive result

The diagnostic action changes the identifiability of the failure. A normal observation can be explained either by open space or by a corrupted sensor. A reference sweep produces an intervention-conditioned observation that separates those hypotheses. The self-model is useful because it selects evidence about the machinery of inference itself.

## What remains unresolved

Both policies receive telemetry designed to expose sensor disagreement, and SA's selector knows the closed set of possible repairs. A harder test would remove the explicit fault channel, introduce novel corruption processes, charge for reference sensing, and learn the experiment-selection policy from chosen actions alone.
