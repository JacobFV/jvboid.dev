The environment remains physically ordinary, but the operator that updates the robot's internal actuator estimate changes. Evidence that previously produced a stable correction now produces an abnormal learning trajectory.

## Failure of causal attribution

SA often raises its predicted future error, which is the right metacognitive signal, but chooses a motor probe rather than an update reset. Its mean utility difference is $-0.106$, with interval $[-0.218,+0.018]$.

The problem is identifiability. Motor drift and update-rule drift can generate similar short-horizon residuals. A semantic self-model does not solve that ambiguity merely by naming both possibilities. The agent needs an experiment whose outcomes differ under the competing causal models—for example, a sandboxed no-motion update, a frozen-parameter probe, or a known calibration trajectory.

This record is evidence against treating articulate internal categories as reliable self-knowledge. The policy can correctly know *that* its future cognition is going wrong while being wrong about *which part* is responsible.
