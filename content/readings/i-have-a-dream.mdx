Worth reading as a piece of construction, not only as a piece of history. The
first half is a prepared legal-financial argument — the promissory note, the
insufficient funds, the fierce urgency of now. Tight, concrete, structurally
conservative.

The part everyone remembers was not in the prepared text. King left the page and
went to material he had used before, and the register changes completely:
anaphora, the prophets, the spiritual at the end. The speech earns that turn
because it spent the first half being precise.

I keep it on the list as a standard for technical writing too. Make the
argument airtight, then say the thing you actually mean.
