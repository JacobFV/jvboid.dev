Two pages, and it reorganizes how you read the last seventy years of AI. Chess,
Go, speech, vision: in each case the hand-engineered representation of what
humans thought they knew lost to search and learning scaled onto more compute.

The bitter part is not that the lesson is wrong-headed to resist — it is that
resisting it feels like doing the real work. Encoding domain knowledge is
satisfying and legibly clever. It is also the thing that gets eaten. I re-read
this whenever I catch myself building a clever prior instead of a scalable
substrate.
